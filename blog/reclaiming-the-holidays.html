Holiday Self-Care

How to Reclaim the Holidays From Stress and Actually Enjoy the Season

A therapist and solo mom on slowing down, protecting what matters, and finding the joy again this year.

This season was never meant to be this stressful, chaotic, overwhelming and exhausting. If holiday stress has you dreading December, you can get the season back by slowing down on purpose, deciding what you want before the calendar decides for you, and making room for whatever the quiet brings up.

Think about where the holidays fall. The days get shorter and darker, colder and calmer. Everything that grew all year sheds and settles, pausing to rest for the season.

So if the holidays are about comfort, stillness, reflection, presence, slowness, magic, family, connection and tradition, why is it so hard to feel any of it?

If only it were that easy. Year after year we remember it’s not. We end up overwhelmed, stressed out, unhappy, and missing the exact things we crave. So how do we do it differently this year?

This used to be me

I didn’t enjoy the season. I missed the reason for it. I rushed through all of it on autopilot. I didn’t realize I needed to do something different to co-create a different experience of the season, so I kept doing the same things and wondering why it all felt the same.

Not anymore.

I’m Alicia. I’m a licensed clinical social worker and holistic therapist in Mesa, and I’m a solo mom raising two boys without a co-parenting partner. I know what December looks like when you’re the one holding every plan, every gift list and every meltdown. And if I can enjoy this season, in all its perfect imperfections, so can you.

That doesn’t mean it’s all twinkle lights. There are still moments of stress and overwhelm, of sadness and grief. But if we wait for perfect before we let ourselves enjoy the holidays, we’ll be waiting forever. This season can still be magical, messy parts included.

So what’s the recipe? There isn’t one. What works for one family doesn’t work for the next, and what worked for you five years ago might not fit your life now. But there are a few pieces I come back to every year, in my office and in my own house.

Slow down on purpose

Slowing down rarely happens by accident in December. You have to go looking for it. Brainstorm it, even. Sit down with a piece of paper and ask yourself three questions.

  • What can I surrender?
  • What can I cancel?
  • Where can I add more rest, relaxation and recharge?

Maybe it’s the third party in one week, the homemade cookies nobody asked for, or the gift exchange you dread every single year. Maybe it’s one evening a week with your phone in another room. Whatever you find, put it on the calendar and protect it the way you’d protect an appointment, because it is one.

Then slow down in your body, not only on your calendar. Unplug. Sit with yourself. Breathe. Feel. Journal. Tune in to you. Meditation, grounding, anchoring and regulation can be as simple as two minutes with your feet flat on the floor, naming five things you can see, and letting each exhale run a little longer than the inhale. These are practices, which means you come back to them again and again, not only when you’re already at the edge. If you’re curious about working with the body this way, you can read how I approach somatic therapy.

Decide what you want before December decides for you

Before you say yes to a single invitation, get to know your own thoughts, feelings and needs for this season. Reflection, introspection, contemplation. Really.

  • What do I want this season to look like?
  • What’s my vision for it?
  • What are my non-negotiables?
  • What are my priorities?

Then ask the important people in your inner circle the same questions. Your partner, your kids, your parents, your closest friends. You might be surprised. The tradition you’ve been wearing yourself out to keep alive might be the one nobody else cares much about, and the thing your kids remember most might be a night on the living room floor with a movie.

Once everyone’s wants are on the table, look for the places you can compromise. And be honest about one more question. What are you attached to that’s creating stuck and struggle in your life?

When the quiet brings up grief or the holiday blues

Making room for what comes up might be the hardest step. When you finally get still, the stillness can show you everything you’ve been avoiding, whether that’s grief, sadness, frustration that it isn’t perfect, loneliness, aloneness, anger or fear.

You can keep avoiding. You can get busy and distracted again, or numb out and tune out. Or you can use part of this season to tune in and process, and acknowledge the thoughts, feelings and beliefs that have been waiting for your attention.

If you’re missing someone this year, or the season stirs up an old loss, you’re not doing the holidays wrong. The holiday blues are real, and grief belongs in this season right alongside the joy.

If what comes up feels bigger than sadness, or you’re having thoughts of hurting yourself, call or text 988 any time to reach the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

Protect your yes

Every yes has a no, and every no has a yes. When you say yes to hosting, you might be saying no to a slow Christmas Eve. When you say no to the third party, you’re saying yes to a quiet night at home with your kids.

So what do you have to say no to so that you can say yes to what really matters? Once you know, say it out loud, kindly and plainly. A few ways it can sound:

  • “We’re keeping Christmas Day small this year, but we’d love to see you on the 27th.”
  • “I can’t host this year. Can I bring dessert instead?”
  • “We’re staying home this year. It’s what our family needs right now.”

You don’t owe anyone a long explanation.

Look for the joy, and notice what’s in the way

Be open to it. Orient to it. Resource it. Create it. Joy rarely shows up while we’re bracing for the next thing, so sometimes we have to go looking for it on purpose.

If you can’t seem to find it, get curious about where you’re stuck. What story, message, belief or feeling state is keeping you from enjoying some of your moments? Maybe it’s “If it isn’t perfect, it doesn’t count.” Maybe it’s “Everyone else’s happiness is my job.” Once you can name the story, you get to decide whether it still runs your holidays.

Rest, honor your capacity, and let plans change

Spend time resting and slowing down. Listen to your capacity, and then honor it. Pivot as you need to, flow as you need to, plan as you need to, and connect with your people.

Don’t be afraid to change plans this season. Sometimes the magic is in the tradition, and sometimes the magic is in something new. If you’re a solo parent or you’re co-parenting across two houses, Christmas morning might land on the 26th this year, and it can still be a real Christmas morning. And don’t be afraid to advocate for not changing plans, either. Keeping a tradition because it matters to you is honoring your capacity too.

Ready to do the holidays differently this year?

There’s no one right way to do the holidays. We do know the wrong way, though. Stressed, overwhelmed, over-extended, overcompensating, people pleasing, and disconnected from the moment and from the people around us.

Let’s make this year different. Figure out what you want. Figure out what the people in your inner circle want. Then protect it as you pivot and flow through the season.

If this season feels heavier than you’d like, or the stillness is bringing up more than you want to sort through on your own, that’s what individual therapy is for. And if your kids’ big feelings are part of what makes December hard, I also offer parenting support. I see clients in person in Mesa and by telehealth across Arizona.
