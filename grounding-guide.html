From Alicia Wright, LCSW

A place to land.

Five things you can do tonight, with nothing but your own body and the room you are sitting in.

You filled something out, which means something in you is already moving. That part deserves a little care while you wait to talk to somebody.

So here is what I would offer you if you were sitting across from me tonight. None of it asks you to understand anything, or to say the right thing, or to feel a certain way about it. It is all body. That is the whole point.

Try one. Not all five. The one that sounds least annoying is usually the right one to start with.

One

Look around the room, slowly.

Let your head turn. Actually turn it, do not just move your eyes. Let your gaze land wherever it wants to land and stay there a second or two before it moves on. A corner, a lamp, the doorway, whatever it picks.

Keep going until you feel something let go. Often it is a breath you did not know you were holding, or your shoulders coming down half an inch.

This one is called orienting, and it is the oldest thing your nervous system knows how to do. When a body is bracing, it stops looking around and starts scanning. Turning your head and letting your eyes rest is how you tell it that nothing here needs watching right now.

Two

Two breaths in, one long breath out.

Breathe in through your nose. Then, without breathing out, sip in a little more air on top of it. Then let it all go slowly through your mouth, and make the exhale longer than both inhales put together.

Do it two or three times. Then stop and let your breathing go back to whatever it wants to do.

The long exhale is the part that matters. That double sip at the top opens the small sacs in your lungs that collapse when you are tense, and the slow release out is the signal your body reads as safe. This is the fastest one here, which is why it is the one to use in a parking lot before you walk in somewhere.

Three

Make a low sound and feel it move.

Take a breath, and on the way out make a low voooo sound, like a foghorn. Low and quiet is fine. Let it run all the way to the end of the breath.

Then stop and notice where you felt the buzz. Chest, throat, belly, back of the sternum. Do it three or four times.

Sound is vibration, and vibration reaches the deep nerve that runs from your brainstem down through your chest and gut. You are not doing anything mystical to yourself here, and you are not doing anything ordinary either. You are using your own voice as a hand on your own insides.

Four

Let the floor hold you.

Put both feet flat down. Press them into the floor like you are about to stand, and hold that press for five or six seconds. Then let it go completely.

Now notice the chair. The place where you meet it, behind your legs and up your back. Let yourself hand it some weight.

A body that has been braced for a long time often forgets it is being held up by anything. Push, release, and then feel what is underneath you. It gives your muscles somewhere to put the effort down.

Five

One hand on your heart, one on your belly.

Warm your hands first if they are cold, rub them together. Then one on the center of your chest, one low on your belly.

Do not try to breathe correctly. Just notice which hand is moving, and how much. Stay there for about a minute.

Touch and warmth and a little weight, from your own hands. Most of us will do this for someone we love and never once do it for ourselves. Notice if this one is harder than the other four. That is worth knowing about yourself, and it is worth bringing in.

If nothing happens

Sometimes nothing happens. Sometimes you do all of it and your chest is exactly as tight as it was before, and there is a voice in there going well, that was pointless.

That is real information, not a failure. A nervous system that has been working hard for years does not put its guard down for a stranger's list on a Tuesday night. It puts it down slowly, with somebody, over time, once it decides you are actually going to stay. That is most of what the work is.

So take what helped. Leave what did not. Bring the rest to the consult and we will start from there.
